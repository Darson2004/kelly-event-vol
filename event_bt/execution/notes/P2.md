## 1. Mechanism

The strategy's net return is computed as
`net = gross − 2·φ·cost_frac − commissions`, where `cost_frac` is the ONE-WAY
quoted half-spread as a fraction of straddle mid and `φ` is the fraction of
that half-spread actually surrendered on each crossing. `engine.py:396`
implements it literally — `fill_px = mid ± φ·half` — applied at entry (buy)
and exit (sell), so a round trip pays `2·φ·half`. **φ was never measured. It
was set to 0.5 because bt.py set it to 0.5.**

The ex-ante economic story, stated before the probes were read, has two parts:

1. **Level.** In a two-sided quoted market with active market makers and
   price improvement, a marketable order does not systematically get mid, and
   a resting order does not systematically get filled. The population of
   prints should therefore cluster somewhere between mid and the touch. φ=0.5
   is the naive midpoint of that range and has no microstructural claim on
   being right; it is a placeholder.
2. **Interaction — the thing that would actually hurt.** The strategy's edge
   concentrates in cheap-vol names, and its cost concentrates in wide markets.
   If φ *rises* with spread width — i.e. in wide markets you not only pay a
   wider spread but a larger *fraction* of it — then the cost model is
   optimistic **precisely where the strategy is already most exposed**, and
   the two errors multiply. The competing microstructure story is the
   opposite: a wide quote is a placeholder that nobody trades against, so
   prints happen well inside it, while a penny-wide quote has no room for
   improvement and everything prints at the touch. That predicts φ *falling*
   with spread width. These two stories make opposite predictions and the
   tape can separate them.

I pre-committed sign `+` on `phi_implied_entry → cost_frac` (probe E0054),
i.e. I bet that story (2)-the-dangerous-one was the live risk. It lost.

There is a third mechanism that no amount of φ measurement touches, and it
turns out to be the real one — see §6.

## 3. Q1 — the φ distribution, and whether 0.5 is defensible

**There are two different φ's and the distinction is the whole answer.**

| | definition | denominator | what it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **φ_A** | `phi_implied_entry` | quoted half-spread **at trade time** | where does volume print relative to the live quote |
| **φ_B** | `eff_half_frac_entry / cost_frac` | the panel's **near-close** half-spread | the multiplier that belongs in `2·φ·cost_frac` |

The backtest charges `2·φ·cost_frac`, and `cost_frac` is a **15:44–15:47
near-close** mark. So **φ_B, not φ_A, is the number the formula needs.**

### Intervals

Computed with the **frozen harness's own `cluster_boot_ci`** (imported from
`exec_harness.py`, not reimplemented; N_BOOT=4000, SEED=20260901, season
clusters). Provenance check: the same call on `cost_frac` reproduces
E0054's `ci95` **exactly** — `[0.01164, 0.01496]`. These are **AUDIT-DIAGNOSTIC
/ not-promotable** because they are not themselves logged to
`experiments.jsonl`; the harness has no command that will emit an interval
for a non-`cost_frac` column.

| quantity | median | 95% CI (season, k=14) | 95% CI (ticker, k=42) | is 0.5 inside? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **φ_A** (tape) | **0.5495** | **[0.5247, 0.5800]** | [0.5156, 0.5867] | **NO — 0.5 is below** |
| **φ_B** (backtest-equivalent) | **0.3964** | **[0.3749, 0.4160]** | [0.3569, 0.4193] | **NO — 0.5 is above** |
| `eff_half_frac_entry` | 0.00490 | [0.00434, 0.00557] | — | — |
| `cost_frac` (fills sub) | 0.01304 | **[0.01164, 0.01496]** ← E0054 | — | — |
| `frac_vol_at_mid` | 0.0769 | [0.0593, 0.0930] | — | — |
| `frac_vol_inside` | 0.6129 | [0.6000, 0.6512] | — | — |

Harness cross-check of φ_A: the E0061–E0066 survival ladder puts the median at
≈0.55, agreeing with the bootstrap median 0.5495 to two decimals.

**So: 0.5 is rejected in both directions, and the two rejections point
opposite ways.** Reconciliation, and the second finding of this cell:

> **E0058: `quoted_half_frac_entry` vs `cost_frac`, IC +0.829.** The
> trade-time quoted half-spread and the near-close quoted half-spread are
> nearly the same variable in rank — `cost_frac` *is* a good proxy — but in
> **level** the near-close mark is **wider**: the median ratio
> (trade-time / near-close) is **0.88**. The 15:44–15:47 snapshot overstates
> the book that trades actually met by ~13%.

φ_A = 0.55 of a book that is 0.88 as wide ⇒ an effective φ_B ≈ 0.40 against
the number the backtest actually multiplies. That is the arithmetic, and it is
the reason the two answers differ.

### Stability

Per-season median φ_A across all 14 fills-era seasons: 0.709, 0.572, 0.551,
0.560, 0.530, 0.547, 0.587, 0.542, 0.512, 0.604, 0.560, 0.487, 0.524, 0.415.
Twelve of fourteen lie in [0.49, 0.61]. The outliers are the first season
(2023Q2, 0.709, partial tcbbo history) and the last (2026Q3, 0.415, n=23,
partial quarter). This is a **stable microstructure constant**, not a
regime-dependent one. That matters: it means a single number is the right
model, and it is worth getting right once.

**Answer to Q1:** 0.5 is *not* inside a defensible interval for either
definition. **Use φ = 0.40** in `2·φ·cost_frac` while `cost_frac` remains a
near-close mark. If the pack ever re-bases `cost_frac` to a trade-time quote,
switch to **φ = 0.55** at the same time — never mix the two.

I pre-committed the dangerous sign (`+`) and it came back **negative on every
measurement**:

| within-ticker demeaned φ_A vs cost_frac | −0.078 | AUDIT-DIAGNOSTIC |

Quintiles of `cost_frac` on the 539 fill events (AUDIT-DIAGNOSTIC):

| quintile | cost_frac | **φ_A** | φ_B | frac vol at mid | frac vol inside | day volume | **eff half paid** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 tightest | 0.0059 | 0.588 | 0.478 | 0.097 | 0.556 | 117 | 0.0026 |
| 2 | 0.0098 | 0.598 | 0.417 | 0.080 | 0.500 | 41 | 0.0042 |
| 3 | 0.0130 | 0.530 | 0.384 | 0.073 | 0.643 | 26 | 0.0051 |
| 4 | 0.0169 | 0.544 | 0.401 | 0.071 | 0.621 | 25 | 0.0064 |
| **5 widest** | **0.0359** | **0.442** | **0.258** | 0.059 | **0.833** | **11** | **0.0099** |

**Ruling out the obvious artifact.** φ_A has the entry-day quote in its
denominator and `cost_frac` is a near-close quote from the same day, so shared
quote noise biases the correlation negative mechanically. Two independent
checks kill that explanation:

1. **Denominator-free features.** `frac_vol_inside_entry` has no spread in it
   at all, and it moves the *same* way: **E0057 +0.180** vs `cost_frac`,
   **E0070 +0.218** vs absolute spread — in wide markets *more* volume prints
   strictly inside the touch, 0.556 → 0.833 across the quintiles. Both signs
   contradicted my prediction, both in the direction that says wide quotes are
   not fully paid.
2. **Instrumented denominator.** Correlating entry-day φ_A against the same
   event's `cost_frac` measured on a **different day** (leads 3/5/7/10 — same
   name and event, no shared quote noise): **−0.087 / −0.112 / −0.145 /
   −0.099**. The negative survives.

**Mechanism, restated with the sign the tape actually chose:** a wide quote is
a placeholder. Nobody trades against a $2.00-wide market at the touch; orders
negotiate inside it, so φ falls. A $0.05-wide market has nothing to improve,
so everything prints at the touch and φ rises toward 1. **φ is a
*decreasing* function of spread width.** The cost model is therefore
pessimistic, not optimistic, exactly where the strategy is most exposed.

**But this does not make wide names safe, and the note must not be read that
way.** The realised one-way toll `eff_half_frac_entry` still rises **3.8×**
across the quintiles (0.0026 → 0.0099), and **E0055 (φ-independent, IC
+0.590)** confirms it: what you pay tracks the quoted spread strongly. Wide
markets are still expensive. They are just not *additionally* penalised
through φ. The φ channel is closed as a risk; the `cost_frac` channel is
untouched and remains EX-1's problem.

**Note on grading:** E0054/E0059 have **no p-value** — the harness cannot
produce one for a cross-sectional statistic. This interaction result
therefore **cannot clear alpha_2 and cannot be promoted**. It is a
*directional refutation of my own pre-committed sign*, which is
evidentially cheap to state (I bet `+`, got `−` on 5 of 5 independent reads)
and expensive to overturn, but it is formally UNGRADED.

## 6. Q4 — is "mid-or-better" literally achievable? **No. Not close.**

Plainly: **no.** On the 537–539 entry days we have tape for:

- Median share of day volume printing **at mid** (±5% of a half-spread):
  **7.7%**, CI [5.9%, 9.3%].
- Only **9.5%** of events (E0067) had ≥ half of the day's volume at mid.
- **55.1%** of events had **less than 10%** of volume at mid (E0069 inverted).
- Median share printing strictly **inside the touch**: 61.3% — so "inside" is
  normal, "at mid" is rare, and "outside" is ~0 (median 0.000).

A rule that says "mid-or-better fills only" describes an event that happens to
7.7% of the volume. As written it is not a policy, it is a wish.

**And the achievability problem is not really about price — it is about size.**
This is the third mechanism from §1, and it is the one that survives:

| | value |
|---|---|
| median entry-day volume, both straddle legs combined | **33 contracts** |
| 10th percentile | **3 contracts** |
| share of events with < 20 contracts total on the day | **38.2%** |
| tcbbo-era lead-14 events with **zero prints on both legs** | **31 / 570 = 5.4%** |
| … with **exactly one leg printing** (the other never traded) | **115 / 570 = 20.2%** |
| … with **≥ 1 leg that never traded on the entry day** | **25.6%** |
| median displayed size at the touch (`touch_sz_entry`) | **79.6 contracts** |
| median individual trade size | **1.25 contracts** |

So: the touch is reasonably **deep** (~80 contracts displayed), but the day is
reasonably **empty** (~33 contracts traded). The measured φ=0.55 is the price
achieved by a **1.25-lot** in a market that does 33 lots a day. Under P4
(1–3% of premium per name), our clip will in many of these names be a
*multiple of the entire day's volume*. **The measured φ is a lower bound on
what we would pay at our size, not an estimate of it.** The one in-sample
hint points the same way, weakly: days whose *median trade* was 3.5 lots show
φ_A = 0.629 vs 0.549 on 1-lot days (Spearman +0.070).

Bracket for a realistic order: **φ ∈ [0.40 (measured, marginal 1-lot), 1.00
(immediate, at the touch, feasible up to ~80 contracts)]**. Which end you land
on is a function of order size and patience, and this dataset cannot resolve
it because tcbbo carries no order-level attribution.

**Honest replacement for the P2 language** (drafted for the orchestrator's T5;
I have not touched `POLICY.md`):

> **P2 — Cost model: φ = 0.40 of the near-close quoted half-spread per
> crossing.** Measured on 539 T−14 entry days, 2023Q2–2026Q3, 42 liquid
> names, from OPRA `tcbbo` prints. Volume-weighted prints land at 0.55 of the
> *trade-time* half-spread; the near-close mark the backtest uses is ~13%
> wider than the trade-time book, giving an effective 0.40 against
> `cost_frac`. The prior 0.5 over-charged the round trip by ~23 bp of
> premium. **"Mid-or-better" is withdrawn as unachievable** — only 7.7% of
> volume prints at mid and only 9.5% of events see half their volume there.
> **Binding size condition:** median entry-day volume in the traded straddle
> is 33 contracts and one leg fails to trade at all on 20% of entry days, so
> φ=0.40 is the price of the marginal 1-lot, not of our clip. Orders must be
> worked with a mid-anchored limit and a touch-crossing backstop; **abort the
> name if the intended clip exceeds ~25% of that contract's recent daily
> volume**, and treat φ=1.0 as the planning assumption for any clip that
> must be completed same-day. **Exit-side φ is UNMEASURED** — no exit-day
> prints matched — so the round-trip restatement assumes symmetry.

## 7. Coverage, staleness (G4), and what this does not generalise to

**Coverage.** 545 of 4,835 panel rows carry entry fills; **539 at lead 14**
(the other 6 at lead 10 are incidental and were excluded by `--lead 14`).
Within the tcbbo era the coverage is high: **539/570 = 94.6%** of lead-14
events from **2023Q2 onward**. Before 2023Q2 the coverage is **0/397** —
`tcbbo` does not exist there (ISSUES BLOCK-1: `data_schema_not_fully_available`
before ~2023).

**This verdict generalises to:** T−14 entries, 42 liquid names, 2023Q2–2026Q3,
near-close-marked ATM straddles, at small size.
**It does not generalise to:** the 2020Q4–2023Q1 half of the strategy's
history (the COVID/2022 vol regimes, where spreads and fill behaviour were
plausibly different and where 45% of the panel's events live); any lead other
than 14; the **exit** crossing; illiquid names (P5 already excludes them); and
any order size materially above a few contracts.

**Selection of the fills subsample.** It is not a random subsample of lead-14
rows — it is an era. Compared with the 421 lead-14 rows without fills, the
fills rows are **cheaper** (`cost_frac` median 1.30% vs 1.66%) and have
slightly larger premium ($15.25 vs $14.49). Some of that is the era, some is
that a contract with zero prints cannot enter the subsample and zero-print
contracts are the wide ones. **The φ estimate is therefore mildly biased
toward the liquid end of an already-liquid universe** — which cuts the same
way as the size caveat and reinforces §6.

## 8. Anomalies found in the machinery (for the orchestrator)

- **ANOM-4 — `require_semantics()` silently passes unasserted columns.** The
  A2 fills join added `n_legs_entry`, `n_trades_entry`, `volume_entry`,
  `quoted_half_frac_entry`, `frac_vol_outside_entry`, `med_trade_size_entry`,
  `touch_sz_entry` with **no `SEMANTICS` entries**. `require_semantics` only
  refuses a column that is *in* `SEMANTICS` and *fails*; an unknown column
  returns an empty result set and is waved through. **G1 can be bypassed by
  testing a column nobody ever asserted.** Two of my own probes (E0058,
  E0060) went through that hole. Recommend: `require_semantics` should refuse
  any column absent from `SEMANTICS`.
- **ANOM-5 — `cmd_test` emits no significance statistic, and its `ci95` is
  the CI of a different quantity.** `stat` is a raw Spearman with no p, no t,
  no MBF; `ci95` is a cluster-bootstrap CI for **median `cost_frac`**
  (`exec_harness.py:481`), printed adjacent to `stat` with no label
  distinguishing them, and identical across every probe on a given subsample.
  Consequence: **no cross-sectional hypothesis in this pack can ever clear an
  alpha threshold**, because it cannot produce a p. Every cross-sectional
  result is structurally UNGRADED. This should be stated in PROTOCOL §4 or
  fixed with a permutation p on the Spearman.
- **ANOM-6 — the fills panel has no exit-side data.** Zero `*_exit` fill
  columns; the `role` pivot produced only `entry`. Every round-trip statement
  in §5 is an entry-side measurement doubled. The exit crossing (1–2 days
  pre-print, elevated IV, crowded) is the plausible worse half and is
  completely unmeasured.
- **ANOM-7 — `test --target stale` is dead code on the active panel.** It
  references `d["stale_dd"]` (`exec_harness.py:490,492`), a column AMEND-1
  removed. It survives only in the selftest fixture (line 739), so **selftest
  passes while the real path raises `KeyError`**. Not run (it would log an
  entry and then crash). This is the same class of defect as ANOM-1: a
  synthetic fixture keeping a broken production path green.

## 9. Committed position

The φ=0.5 assumption was never defensible as a *statement*, and it is
approximately right as a *number* — but for a reason nobody intended, and with
the residual risk sitting somewhere entirely different from where P2 put it.
Where volume actually prints is **0.55 of the contemporaneous half-spread**
(CI [0.525, 0.580]), so as a description of fill quality 0.5 is too kind and
"mid-or-better" is fiction: only 7.7% of volume touches mid, and in 9 of 10
events the majority of volume does not. But the backtest does not multiply the
contemporaneous half-spread — it multiplies a **15:44–15:47 near-close mark
that is ~13% wider than the book trades met** (E0058, IC +0.829 in rank, 0.88
in level). Those two errors run opposite ways and the wider one wins: the
multiplier that belongs in `2·φ·cost_frac` is **0.40** (CI [0.375, 0.416]),
so the incumbent 0.5 has been **over-charging** the strategy by ~23 bp of
premium per round trip, +0.23pp on the +6.39%. My pre-committed fear — that φ
would be worse in wide markets and compound the cost exposure — is refuted on
five independent reads including two denominator-free ones and an
instrumented-denominator one; the tape says the opposite, because wide quotes
are placeholders that get negotiated inside (frac-inside 0.56 → 0.83 across
cost quintiles) while penny-wide quotes leave nothing to improve. So the φ
*channel* is closed as a risk. What is **not** closed, and what P2 was
accidentally protecting against, is **size**: φ=0.40 is the price paid by a
1.25-lot on a day when the entire straddle trades 33 contracts, one leg does
not trade at all on 20% of entry days, and 38% of events see under 20
contracts total. The honest re-statement of P2 is not a fill-price rule at all
— it is a **capacity** rule.

**Position.** Set **φ = 0.40** against near-close `cost_frac` (equivalently
0.55 against a trade-time quote); the incumbent 0.5 over-charges the round
trip by **22.6 bp of premium at the median trade (+3.5% of the +6.39%)**, φ
does **not** degrade in wide markets (it falls: IC −0.122 vs `cost_frac`,
−0.209 vs absolute spread), and **"mid-or-better" must be withdrawn** — 7.7%
of volume prints at mid — and replaced by a size/capacity condition, because
the unquantified risk in P2 is our clip against a 33-contract day, not our
price against the quote.

**Confidence: medium-high (~75%)** — high on the φ *level* and its sign
against spread width (17 logged probes, 5 independent reads of the
interaction all negative, 12 of 14 seasons within [0.49, 0.61], intervals
that exclude 0.5 under both season and ticker clustering); dropped below 80%
because **the exit leg is entirely unmeasured**, the harness could produce
**no p-value for the interaction**, and the sample is one era (2023Q2+) of a
panel that is 45% pre-2023.

**Boundary conditions.** Holds for T−14 entries, 42 liquid names, 2023Q2–
2026Q3, ATM straddles marked near the close, **at small size** (order ≲ a few
contracts, or ≲ 25% of the contract's recent daily volume). Does **not** hold
for: the exit crossing (unmeasured); pre-2023 regimes; illiquid names; any
clip that is a material fraction of daily volume — for those, plan on φ→1.0.
Also does not hold if `cost_frac` is ever re-based to a trade-time quote, in
which case the correct constant becomes 0.55, not 0.40.

**What would flip this.**
1. **Exit-day tcbbo** (the missing purchase) showing φ_B on the exit crossing
   ≥ **0.60** — that alone erases the entry-side conservatism and returns the
   round trip to ~neutral or worse. This is the single highest-value next buy
   and it is cheap: exit days for the same 539 events.
2. A **size-conditioned** φ — recompute restricting to prints ≥ 10 contracts.
   If φ_B on large prints is ≥ **0.50**, the 0.40 recommendation dies and the
   incumbent 0.5 is vindicated as a size-aware compromise. (tcbbo carries
   `size`, so this is computable from data already bought — it needs a
   harness amendment, not a purchase.)
3. Pre-2023 coverage showing median φ_A outside [0.49, 0.61] in the earlier
   regimes, which would make φ a regime variable rather than a constant and
   invalidate a single-number policy.
4. Any probe recovering a **positive** φ↔spread IC with a denominator-free
   feature; that would resurrect the compounding-cost fear I bet on and lost.

**Evidence weight.** 17 of 17 probes logged (E0054–E0070). On the φ level:
6 of 6 harness reads agree (survival ladder E0061–E0066 crosses 0.5 at
t≈0.55; bootstrap median 0.5495) — effect size: 0.5 sits **below** the whole
φ_A season-clustered interval and **well above** the φ_B interval, under both
season and ticker clustering. On the interaction: **5 of 5 support the
negative sign** (E0054 −0.122, E0059 −0.209, E0057 +0.180 and E0070 +0.218 on
frac-inside — both denominator-free and both meaning "less fully paid when
wider" — plus the instrumented −0.087/−0.112/−0.145/−0.099 and ticker-level
−0.318), **0 of 5 support my pre-committed `+`**. On achievability: 3 of 3
admit probes agree that mid fills are rare (9.5% / 23.5% / 44.9% at the 0.50 /
0.25 / 0.10 thresholds). **Zero probes clear alpha_2 = 0.015198** and none
needed to — see the verdict.

## 10. Verdict

### **REFUTED** — for P2 as written, and **MEASURED** for the constant beneath it.

- **"Mid-or-better fills only" is REFUTED as a description of reality.** Only
  **7.7%** of entry-day volume prints at mid (CI [5.9%, 9.3%]); only **9.5%**
  of events see half their volume there; **55%** of events see under a tenth.
  The rule cannot be satisfied and must be withdrawn from `POLICY.md`.
- **The φ=0.5 constant is MEASURED and should be regraded, not deleted.** The
  defensible value is **0.40** against near-close `cost_frac`
  (CI [0.375, 0.416]) / **0.55** against a trade-time quote
  (CI [0.525, 0.580]). Recommended grade: **MEASURED-ON-COST**, the same
  grade P1 carries, for the same reason — the cost side is measured on our
  own tape, the payoff side is not independently established.
- **The φ↔spread-width interaction is UNGRADED, not SUPPORTED.** The point
  estimates are unambiguous and unanimous, but `cmd_test` emits no p-value
  (ANOM-5), so nothing here can clear alpha_2 = 0.015198 and nothing should
  be promoted to CANDIDATE on it. Record it as a **measurement with a
  refuted pre-committed sign**.

### On alpha

**No alpha need be spent on this cell.** The primary deliverable is the
calibration of an input constant that was previously asserted without
evidence — an estimation problem, not a discovery test. This mirrors the
tick-1 precedent: P1's 17.7bp was a *measurement*, graded MEASURED-ON-COST.
The sequential budget prices the risk of manufacturing a spurious *effect*;
it does not price replacing a guessed constant with a measured one. If the
orchestrator disagrees and books the interaction as a finding, then it must
be booked as **failing** alpha_2, because no p exists for it.

### Detectable-effect floor (for the parts that ARE null)

- **Interaction:** at n=537 with 14 season clusters, a Spearman of this
  sample size resolves roughly |IC| ≳ 0.10. A *positive* φ↔cost IC larger
  than **+0.10** would have been visible and is excluded; anything between
  −0.10 and +0.10 in the *positive* direction is below the floor. The observed
  −0.122 sits just outside it, on the opposite side of my prediction.
- **φ level:** the season-clustered interval half-width on median φ_A is
  ±0.028, so a true φ_A differing from 0.55 by more than **±0.03** would
  have been detected; on φ_B the half-width is ±0.021, so **±0.02**. Both
  intervals exclude 0.5, comfortably.
- **Round-trip toll:** the restatement resolves a toll difference of about
  **±17 bp** (half the E0054 cost_frac CI width). The measured 22.6 bp
  correction is only modestly above that floor — **this is the number to be
  humble about**, and it is why the §5 delta is reported as "≈23 bp, roughly
  one commission," not as a precise revision of the headline.
