# H4 — regime-macro / month (calendar month of ann_date), predicted sign "−"

**Status: EXPLORATORY cell, weak mechanism (flagged as such in HYPOTHESES.yaml at registration).**

## 1. Mechanism

The ex-ante story: January–April prints coincide with full-year reporting
season, when companies issue or reset annual guidance. Guidance resets add a
second source of true event variance on top of the quarterly beat/miss, so
early-calendar events should have fatter realized-surprise tails than the
implied pricing (which is calibrated to "typical" quarters) reflects. Lower
month number → higher big-winner probability → negative tail-rate diff on
the above-median-month half.

Stated honestly: this is a **weak mechanism**. It assumes implied-move
pricing systematically fails to season-adjust, which sophisticated vol
sellers have every incentive to do, and it was registered as exploratory
for exactly that reason. Mechanism-free-adjacent; do not promote on
discovery evidence alone.

**Pre-registered confound (binding on this note):** discovery spans only 5
earnings seasons (2024Q2–2025Q2). Cross-tab of month × season (descriptive,
logged below) shows 11 of 12 calendar months map to exactly ONE season —
only June appears twice (n=8 in 2024Q2, n=28 in 2025Q2). "Month" is
therefore not an independent feature in this sample; it is a relabeling of
season. See section 3.

## 3. Is any month effect distinguishable from season clustering? No.

This was the mandatory question for this cell, and the answer is no, on
three grounds:

1. **Structural confound.** Month → season is (except 36 June events) a
   many-to-one function onto the 5 seasons. The harness's median split at
   month=8 puts {2024Q4, Sep-2024} in the high half (n=971) and everything
   else in the low half (n=1921). Any "month" statistic here is a season
   contrast in disguise, and the season-cluster bootstrap correctly treats
   it as one — with only 5 clusters the bootstrap-t distribution is wide,
   which is why even the IC at −0.046 gets p=0.136. That p is the honest
   one; an iid test on the same numbers would fake significance
   (Kolari–Pynnonen over-rejection, exactly what the harness exists to
   prevent).

2. **The descriptive season pattern points the WRONG way for the
   mechanism.** From the `describe` output: winner rates by season are
   2024Q3 = 4.1%, 2024Q4 = 2.8%, 2025Q1 = 1.1%, 2025Q2 = 2.4%. The only
   early-calendar (Jan–Mar, guidance-reset) season in the sample, 2025Q1,
   has the LOWEST tail rate — a quarter of the mid-year 2024Q3 rate. If
   anything, this one pass through the calendar contradicts H4's direction.
   The mildly negative IC (X0022) is driven by 2025-half-year returns
   ranking below 2024-half-year returns generally, i.e. a regime/vintage
   drift, not a reporting-season tail effect.

3. **The registered statistic is degenerate under calendar wraparound.**
   Month is circular; a linear median split on it mixes Jul–Aug 2024 (the
   hottest season) into the "low-month" half alongside 2025Q1 (the
   coldest), mechanically cancelling toward zero — which is what X0012
   shows. Even a strongly significant result on this feature would have
   been unattributable to calendar month vs. the specific vol regime of
   that quarter; one calendar cycle cannot separate them.

## 4. Committed position

The three probes add up to: nothing detectable, and what little directional
texture exists in the sample runs against the registered mechanism. The
tail-rate difference is two orders of magnitude below the detectable floor;
the IC's mild negativity is a 2024-vs-2025 vintage contrast that the
5-cluster bootstrap rightly refuses to bless; and the single Jan–Mar season
we observe has the thinnest tails in the panel, not the fattest. This cell
should not be registered for validation, and "month" should not reappear as
a feature until the panel spans at least 2–3 full calendar cycles so that
month and season decorrelate.

- **Position:** Calendar-month has no detectable effect on big-winner
  (net ≥ +50%) rates in small-cap discovery; any true month effect is below
  a ±2pp tail-rate difference at n=2892 with 5 season clusters, and the
  guidance-reset mechanism is descriptively contradicted by the one
  early-calendar season observed (2025Q1 at 1.1% vs 2024Q3 at 4.1%).
- **Confidence:** medium (50–80%) that there is no exploitable month
  effect of the registered form — the null is clean across all 3 probes,
  but one calendar cycle cannot logically rule out a genuine seasonal
  pattern; the confidence ceiling here is structural, not statistical.
- **Boundary conditions:** holds for small-cap events, 2024Q2–2025Q2,
  tail defined as long_ret_net ≥ +50%, month tested as a linear
  median-split feature. Says nothing about multi-year seasonality, about
  the liquid universe, or about month interacted with a pricing feature
  (e.g., is IM cheap relative to season norms) — none of those were tested.
- **What would flip this:** a panel with ≥3 January–April seasons showing
  a season-cluster-robust tail-rate elevation ≥ +2pp in Jan–Apr vs rest
  with consistent sign in each year; or the sealed validation set
  (2025H2+) showing the 2025Q1 low-rate reading was the anomaly and a
  Jan–Apr fat-tail pattern reasserting.
- **Evidence weight:** 0 of 3 probes support an actionable effect (3 of 3
  match the predicted sign, but at effect sizes −0.08pp tail / −0.046 IC /
  −0.023 median with p = 0.95 / 0.14 / 0.47, MBF 0.999 / 0.49 / 0.92 —
  sign-matching at these magnitudes is noise, not support).

## Verdict: NULL-WITH-POWER

We could have detected a tail-rate difference of roughly ±2pp between month
halves at this n (2892 events, ~971/1921 split, 2.7% base rate, 5 season
clusters); we observed −0.08pp. Effect, if any, is below that floor — and
even a detected effect would have been unattributable to month vs. season
in a one-calendar-cycle sample. Recommend: do not register for Stage 5
validation; revisit only when the panel spans multiple years.
