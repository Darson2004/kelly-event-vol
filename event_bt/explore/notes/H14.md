## 0. Usable n / coverage (reported FIRST per the registered expectation)

The ratio algebraically cancels im_entry:
`ratio_last_im/ratio_avg_im = (im/last_im)/(im/avg_im) = avg_im/last_im` —
the exact implied-side twin of H7's winning form `avg_rm/last_rm`. It needs
BOTH prior-implied columns, so coverage is bounded by the sparser one
(`ratio_avg_im`, n=474 per H1). Harness-reported usable **n = 474 of 2892
(16.4%)** — the documented H1 implied-history degeneracy, a selected
older/longer-optioned subpopulation. 474 clears the harness split floor
(≥10 per side) and supports the tail statistic, so the cell is NOT BLOCKED;
but at 2.7% base rate that is only ~13 expected winners, so the power floor
is fat (quantified in §3).

## 1. Mechanism

Implied-side recency anchoring: high `avg_im/last_im` means the market priced
the name's LAST event below the name's own implied norm. If the R1/R2 (H7)
recency-anchoring effect is a GENERAL recency phenomenon — markets anchor on
whatever the last observation was — then a last-event implied markdown should
also predict tail winners, sign "+". If instead H7 is SPECIFIC to anchoring on
the last REALIZED print (a quiet realized move drags expectations down while
true move magnitude mean-reverts), this cell should be null: last-event
implied pricing carries no mean-reversion information of its own. The cell
was registered explicitly so that EITHER outcome informs the locked R1/R2
mechanism.

## 3. Committed position

The evidence adds up to: **the implied side carries no recency signal, and
that cuts FOR the realized-move-specific reading of R1/R2 — H7 is anchoring
on the last realized print, not generic recency, and it is not an artifact of
the last/avg ratio construction.** The identically-constructed implied-side
ratio, on the same panel with the same grader, comes back null on all three
statistics and — more informatively — wrong-signed on all four probes
(tail -0.84pp, IC -0.035, median -1.5pp). Power honesty: at n=474 with a
2.7% base, the perm-governed detectable tail floor is ~2x binomial SE ≈ 3pp,
so a full H7-sized +2pp lift sits at the edge of that floor and its absence
alone would be weak evidence. What makes this more than a power failure is
the sign: if the implied side carried an attenuated H7-type effect of +2pp,
a negative point estimate would occur only ~10% of the time (effect 2pp vs
binomial SE ~1.5pp); we observed negative estimates on ALL of tail, ic, and
median, under both p machineries. The construction-artifact alternative
("any avg/last ratio finds winners mechanically") is dead: same construction,
nothing there. Consistency check: the mild negative lean fits H1's separate
finding that implied CHEAPNESS vs own history helps — a low last-im is a
form of implied markdown persisting into entry — but none of the negative
stats here clears even p=0.36, so I do not claim a reverse effect.

- **Position:** `avg_im/last_im` (the implied-side recency ratio) has no
  positive tail effect above a ~3pp detectable floor at n=474 — the point
  estimate is -0.84pp, wrong-signed — and therefore the locked R1/R2 (H7)
  effect should be interpreted as REALIZED-move anchoring specifically
  (quiet last print -> violent snap-back), not general recency anchoring.
- **Confidence:** medium (~65%). 4 of 4 probes agree in sign and nullity,
  two p machineries agree, and the sign argument survives the fat power
  floor; docked below high because n=474 (~13 winners) makes any single
  tail split noisy, and the implied-history subsample (16% of the panel,
  older names) plus the low dispersion of implied moves (last_im hugs
  avg_im far more than realized moves do) could attenuate a small true
  effect into the noise.
- **Boundary conditions:** holds only on the implied-history subpopulation
  (n_prior_im sufficient for both ratios; 474 of 2892 events), small-cap
  discovery panel 2024Q2–2025Q2. Says nothing about liquid names, where
  implied history is dense and dispersion may differ — the isolation
  conclusion should be re-checked there before it hardens into doctrine.
  The R1/R2 implication is an interpretation transfer, not a test of R1/R2
  itself: H7's own numbers are untouched by this result.
- **What would flip this:** (1) the same feature on tail coming back
  positive at >= +2pp with perm p <= 0.05 on the sealed validation set or on
  liquid historical (dense implied history = real power) — that would revive
  the general-recency reading; (2) an auditor demonstration that within the
  n=474 subsample the H7 realized-side effect ALSO vanishes — that would
  convert this from "implied-side null" to "coverage-selected subsample
  kills everything," gutting the isolation inference (this is the single
  biggest caveat: I could not condition the realized ratio on implied
  coverage through the frozen harness).
- **Evidence weight:** 0 of 4 probes support the registered "+"; 4 of 4
  point weakly negative, none significant (best p=0.36); effect sizes
  observed: tail -0.84pp on a 2.7% base, IC -0.035, median -1.5pp vs the
  realized-side benchmark of +2.04pp (t=3.26) at comparable n.

## 4. Verdict

**NULL-WITH-POWER** — the implied-side recency effect, if any, is below the
~3pp tail-rate detectable floor of the governing perm statistic at n=474
(and below the ~1.5pp floor implied by the season-cluster bootstrap SE of
0.66pp from X0064); we could have seen an H7-sized +2pp lift as a positive,
likely-borderline-significant estimate, and instead saw -0.84pp with the
wrong sign on every probe. Not BLOCKED: usable n=474 cleared the harness
floors and both p machineries returned finite, well-behaved statistics.
For R1/R2: this outcome supports the realized-move-specific mechanism and
argues against both the general-recency and the ratio-construction-artifact
alternatives.
